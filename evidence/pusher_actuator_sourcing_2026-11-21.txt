(c) Higher-current motor run at reduced current
   Force scales roughly with current below saturation. At 0.7 A rms:
     Nanotec LGA351S12-A-UIAP-038 (1.2 A): ~140 N low speed, ~128 N at 5 mm/s, ~115 N at 8 mm/s. [INFERENCE] MEETS 120 N at <= 5 mm/s, thin margin.
     Nanotec LGA351S12-A-TJBA-038 (1.2 A, 1 mm): ~116 N at 1.7 mm/s. Fails.
     OMC 1.5 A captive parts: ~70 N. Fails.
   Side benefit: copper loss 0.49 x 1.85 x 2 = 1.8 W vs 5.3 W at rated current - cooler carriage.
   FFC check: driver current setting is rms; sine peak at 0.7 A rms = 0.99 A, i.e. 0.49 A peak / 0.35 A rms per
   conductor with leads doubled. If the 0.35 A FFC limit is a thermal (rms) rating this is exactly at the limit;
   if it is a peak rating, use 0.65 A rms (or check the FFC datasheet). [INFERENCE]

(d) Geared or belt-and-screw
   e.g. a 0.5-0.6 A NEMA 11/14 rotary motor with 2:1 belt or small planetary into a 1-2 mm lead screw and a guided
   pusher. Gets force at low current, but adds backlash (gearbox) or belt compliance, a second bearing set, a guide,
   ~50-100 g and more build steps. Not recommended for a builder-friendly machine unless (c) and (e) both fail.

(e) Change the cable instead
   To carry a 1.2 A winding at rated current (sine peak 1.7 A): at 0.35 A per conductor, 4 conductors per motor lead
   (16 for the pusher) instead of 2 (8 now). For a 1.5 A winding: 5 per lead (20 conductors).
   Alternatives: a 1.0 mm pitch FFC (typical ratings ~1 A per conductor - check the specific cable datasheet, not
   verified here) with 2 conductors per lead; or route the pusher motor on its own small 4-core high-flex round cable
   in the drag chain. Benefit: Nanotec UIAP at 1.2 A gives ~220-241 N (~2x margin) with no part change.

=====================================================================================================
RESOLUTION FOR 0.5 uL STEPS (10 uL pipette)
=====================================================================================================
Plunger travel per uL depends on the pipette and is not known here. A 0.5 uL step needs plunger travel
d = 0.5 / (uL per mm). Even for a very short-stroke pipette at 20 uL/mm, d = 25 um.
  Nanotec UIAP 0.6096 mm: 3.048 um/full step, 0.1905 um/(1/16) -> 25 um = 8.2 full steps.
  Nanotec TJBA 1 mm:      5.000 / 0.3125 um.
  HK K 1.2192 mm:         6.096 / 0.381 um -> 25 um = 4.1 full steps.
  HK J 2.4384 mm:         12.19 / 0.762 um.
  MOONS E06008 0.79 mm:   3.97 / 0.248 um.
  Nanotec LGA28 UGAQ 0.635 mm: 3.175 / 0.198 um.
  OMC 2.54 mm: 12.7 / 0.79 um; OMC 6.35 mm: 31.75 / 1.98 um.
Step size is not the limit for any fine-lead option; axial play (+/-0.05-0.06 mm Nanotec, not published for HK),
screw/nut compliance and seal friction will dominate repeatability. Use full steps or 1/16 with the expectation that
microstep positions under load are not accurate to the microstep.

=====================================================================================================
MEASURE ON THE BENCH BEFORE COMMITTING (bench rig units)
=====================================================================================================
1. The real load: plunger force vs travel for the chosen 10 uL pipette (first stop, second stop/blow-out, tip ejection
   if the pusher does it), with a load cell. The 120 N target itself should be confirmed.
2. Push force vs current (0.5, 0.6, 0.7 A rms) and speed (1, 3, 5, 8 mm/s) at 24 V on the actual driver and
   microstep setting, against a load cell, until stall. Accept only if >= 1.3x the measured plunger load at 0.7 A.
3. Lost motion and repeatability: dial indicator on the pusher tip, bidirectional and unidirectional, with the
   pipette spring loading it; then gravimetric 0.5 uL steps on the 10 uL pipette (n >= 10 per volume).
4. Temperatures: motor case and the FFC at the duty cycle of a full run (0.7 A, realistic hold current).
5. Missed steps: run the full dispense profile repeatedly with step-loss detection (encoder or index check).
6. End-stop behaviour: confirm homing method never loads the internal stop above Nanotec's 0.57 A limit.
7. Fit: carriage clearance front and rear from the STEP model; added mass on the carriage axes.
8. Life: screw/nut wear at working load over a representative number of cycles (Nanotec publishes a rated force
   much lower than peak for the 1 mm thread; ask Nanotec for the UIAP rated force and expected life).
