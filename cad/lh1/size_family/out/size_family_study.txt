=================================================================

Outputs: size_family.json (every number below), layouts.yaml (parameter sets and layouts), checks_S.json (interference/reach),
         solver_result.json (layout search), plan_S.png, plan_M.png, plan_L.png

ASSUMED limits used throughout

- Reach: a person reaches comfortably about 600 mm in from the door.

0. HOW MUCH HARDER IS IT (the short answer)
-------------------------------------------
Not much, if growth is restricted to one direction (depth, Y) and the rest of the machine is built once at final size. Then a
size change touches about 20 parts, all of them cheap, symmetric left/right pairs or flat sheets, none of them in the X
stiffness path; the gantry, head, Z height, dock, door, front and back panels, drawer, base front/back and electronics are the
same part numbers in every size. Lateral stiffness, X dynamics and exchange time are identical across the family (they depend
on X, which does not change); Y gets slightly better as it gets shorter. The extra cost of building the starter so that it can
grow is about GBP 150-300 and 18 mm of width (section 3.4). The extension kit S->M costs about GBP 60 in members plus the new
stations, and 3-4 working days of downtime, most of it re-commissioning, much of which the head camera and deck markers do.

It is a lot harder if the machine also has to grow in width (X): that means a new X beam (the stiffness-critical part,
torsion grows with length, vertical bending with length cubed), new X rails, new belt, re-squaring, plus a new door, front,
back, roof and base cross members. Do not offer X growth.

Two things in this study are new and matter whether or not the family is adopted:
(a) Revision L has an undetected station-to-station overlap: the shaker body at D4 overlaps the D3 lid park insert (3,364 mm3)
    and its lid stack (21,844 mm3). machine_l only checks overlaps across groups; all stations are one group. The shaker body is
    148 mm wide on a 132 mm pitch, centred. Fix: set the shaker 8 mm outward and cut the shelf to x 350 (done in the family), or
    leave the column-3 neighbour empty.
(b) Five placement rules found by running machine_l's interference engine on a first S layout (section 2.2: N1-N5). They are not
    in the K2 rule checker and every future layout needs them.


1. WHAT IS CHEAP TO EXTEND AND WHAT IS NOT
-----------------------------------------
Direction: grow in DEPTH (Y) only, by inserting one standard row (110 mm) in front of the back row G. Rows F, A, B (front: the
door, drawer, tip chute, thermocycler, tube blocks) never move; row G (tip frames), the dock and the back frame move back 110 mm
as one section.

Why Y and not X:
- The X beam carries the head; it is the largest term in the lateral budget after the carriage plates (X beam torsion 0.062 mm
  of 0.319 mm at 150 N, CALCULATED, rev L). Torsion grows with beam length, vertical bending with length cubed. The Y side
  beams sit on uprights and carry no lateral term at all.
- An X change alters about 12 different parts (beam, two X rails, X belt, door, front panel, lower front, back panel, roof,
  base front/back, base cross members, drawer width); a Y change alters about 8 kinds of part, nearly all in left/right pairs.
- Y is the dimension the bench limits, so the starter must be short in Y anyway; it does not need to be narrow.

Notes on the rows above
- Stiffness: the horizontal (lateral) terms are set by X, the gantry, the carriage and the coupling, so every size has the same
  0.32 mm at 150 N; the X belt EA 3e4 still fails the dwell (1.29 s) in every size, so the 6e4 belt requirement stands. The
  vertical term falls with the shorter side-beam spans. Lateral margin left at 150 N: 0.096 (S), 0.085 (M), 0.072 (L) mm.
- Ethanol: the fan and the airflow switch are the same on every size and are 1.4-2.8 x the K = 5 need, so with the fan
  RUNNING all sizes are equivalent. With the fan STOPPED the starter reaches the lower flammable limit 20 % sooner (0.85 h
  instead of 1.06 h at the faster evaporation rate, open troughs). That is the only safety figure that gets worse with size;
  it argues for the ER-1 slotted covers and the "no proven flow -> no ethanol step" rule, which already exist. Basis: deck_k
  method (competent-person review still required); the deck_k K2 figure of 0.68 m3 was an estimate before the 3D frame.
  Above-deck-only basis gives S 1.42 / 0.71 h.
- Mass: rev L modelled parts scaled by depth and width; deck modules pro rata (ASSUMED); fasteners/electrics 17.5 kg ASSUMED.
  Family L is heavier than rev L because it carries three more places and the full drawer is counted in both.
- Tips: S has no tip parking, so its figure is the planner's no-parking count (rev K corrected counts: p10 336, p200 600,
  p300 968; p1000 75 ASSUMED as 300 x 24/96). The tip rules that need no parking (same tips into empty wells, mix first,
  washes capped at 150 uL) still apply on S. M and L carry tip parking and use the waste-tip-per-well rule (p300 1,136 at 48
  and 2,288 at 96). p1000 in S and M: one person-loaded rack of 96 (M needs 150: one reload, a named lower figure under
  ADR-009).

3. THE EXTENSION KITS
---------------------
3.1 S -> M (insert row C, 110 mm)
Replace: Y rails x2 (741 -> 851), Y side beams x2, Y belts x2, Y chain tray.
Extend in place: base side and base long members x4 (+110 piece, joining plates), roof sides x2, side panels x2 and roof (one
strip each), shelf (one segment, rails spliced), Y chain (links), harness (extension leads at the back-section connector plane).
Add: deck strip plate for row C + support bar + 2 posts; stations TPK-1, ATR-2, lid park 2 (moves from the shelf), magnet 2;
one tower; H2 hand-off nest and shutter (the opening is already cut in the right panel if fix-now item 9 is followed).
Re-use: everything else, including the whole gantry with its head, the dock, the drawer, the front, the door and all S stations
in the same places.
Steps: (1) park the head, remove tools to a rack, unplug the back-section connectors; (2) remove the back panel, the side panels'
back strips and the roof strip; (3) lift the gantry off (two people; Y moving mass about 22 kg) or slide it to the front on its
blocks with the new rails beside; (4) unbolt the back section (row-G strip, dock rails, back corner uprights) and move it back
110 mm on the extended base members; (5) fit the new side beams and Y rails, align with the rail jig; (6) refit the gantry,
fit the longer belts; (7) fit the row-C strip, bar and posts, the new stations; (8) panels, roof strip, harness leads.
Re-commission:
- Y rail parallelism and gantry squareness: by hand with the jig; the head camera then checks it by measuring the place markers
  at both deck corners (a skew shows as a rotation; ADR-004 reports > 1 mm as a fault, it does not correct it).
- Y belt tension: by hand, frequency method; Y endstop/travel limit and machine config: edit (size config file).
- Input shaping / resonance: automatic with the accelerometer (fix-now item 7).
- Deck places: AUTOMATIC: the camera measures every place's two machined holes and proposes the calibration (new row and the
  moved row G); deck plate step at the new joint shows here.
- Dock bays: semi-automatic if each bay saddle carries a marker (fix-now item 8); otherwise teach 8 bays by hand. Then a short
  exchange test: 20 exchanges per carriage with the code/latch/bay-switch checks.
- Planner: the M deck/machine configuration (layouts.yaml) replaces S's.
- Safety: door interlock unchanged (door unchanged); extraction airflow switch re-proved; enclosure leak check at the new laps.
Downtime ASSUMED: mechanical 1.5-2 days (two people), commissioning about 1 day, one validation run 1 day: 3-4 working days.
Parts: members about GBP 61 (rev H rates) + the stations (not priced in the repository: TPK-1 printed/sheet, bought low-elution
magnet plate, ATR-2 printed rack).
3.2 M -> L: the same kit with row D; adds balance, weighing nest, ATR-1, second plate stack, p1000 rack in column 0, four more
towers; members about GBP 61; same downtime.

 1. X at final width: X beam 1,185 mm, X rails, X belt, gantry, both Y carriages, door, front panels, back panel, roof width,

3.4 The premium the starter pays for being extendable (ESTIMATE)
- Width: a starter that could never grow could drop column 0 and the outer shelf column: about 150 mm narrower (about 1,145
  wide). Its X beam would be about 150 mm shorter: lateral deflection at 150 N about 0.008 mm smaller, X belt mode a little
  higher. Not worth having given the bench limit is depth, not width.
- Parts: X-length parts at full width, a second 600 W supply, a dock base for 8 bays, connector plane and leads, per-bay panels:
  about GBP 150-300 (rev H rates; the 600 W supply GBP 48, the X beam tube about GBP 0.32/mm).
- Mass: about 3 kg.


4. GROWING WITHOUT GROWING THE GANTRY: SIDE MODULES
---------------------------------------------------
The gantry cannot carry the gripper outside its frame (the Y carriages are at the frame sides). Everything outside is reached
through the two edge hand-off nests (OF, OA; x 540) behind shuttered openings in the right panel; a side module must bring the
item to the nest itself with its own carrier (deck_k: 600 x 600 mm bench beside the panel, carrier reaching 70 mm through).
- Tip supply tower with its own lift: refill wafers (or racks) delivered to H1, picked by the gripper as from a shelf tower.
  Needs a lift and a carrier (two axes, custom), a wafer-present sensor, its own supply, an interlocked shutter, a planner
  device. This is the single module that moves S furthest: tips for 96 samples (5,204) are 54 wafers; S's shelf holds 29.
- Plate hotel: lidded plates/plate stacks delivered to H1/H2; needs the same carrier class; useful from 48 samples up.
- Bulk reagents: a dispense nozzle in spare dock bay 7 or 8 (the cable bay 8 already has a route) fed by tubing from bottles
  outside, with a bought peristaltic or syringe pump; reduces reservoir places and refills. Ethanol from outside also lowers
  the ethanol load inside.
- Waste: LW-1 already drains to bottles outside the working volume; lengthening the tubes to carboys beside the machine is
  trivial. Tip waste: the 29 L crate already holds about 10,900 tips.
How far towards 96 does this get S: tips, bulk liquids and waste, yes; the deck, no. At 96 the binding limits in the planner
(rev K figures) are per-sample sources on the cold carrier (12 strip columns for one index set alone), reservoir columns and
plates in parallel, which are deck places. S with side modules is a 24- to perhaps 48-sample machine (ESTIMATE, not
re-planned); 96 needs the L deck.


5. WHAT MODULARITY MAKES HARDER OR RISKIER (bluntly)
----------------------------------------------------
- Alignment at joints: the only precision joints are the Y rails. Replacing them costs about GBP 28 a row and an afternoon;
  jointing them saves that and adds a permanent step under the gantry. Do not joint rails or the beams that carry them.
- Part numbers and build documents: 3 sizes x about 20 size-dependent parts = about 60 variants of cut-lengths and sheets.
  Controlled by generating them from one parameter (rows) as this module does; the build guide gets one table per size, not
  three guides.
- Belt tuning per size: Y belt length and tension, input shaping and Y limits per size; X unchanged. Small.
- Safety case per size: enclosure volume (LFL time with the fan off, section 2), extraction proof, door interlock (unchanged),
  heater/thermal chain (unchanged). One assessment written with the volume as the variable, and the starter as the worst case.
- Planner and firmware: a deck file, travel limits and a dock y per size, and the S-only rule "p1000 tips on carriage B".
  Data, not code (ADR-009 rule 6).
- Layout fragility: the rules N1-N5 show a layout that looks fine on the K2 checker can still collide; every size needs the
  CAD run. That is the real cost of a family and the reason to keep the number of sizes at three.
- Segmented frame that bolts on in sections versus replace-the-long-members: a bolt-on section would need its own Y rail
  segment (a joint under the blocks), a spliced side beam (a hinge in the largest vertical term), a door and roof that span the
  joint, and an extraction seal at the joint, for an upgrade done once or twice in the machine's life. Replacing the long
  members costs about GBP 61 a step and gives a machine with no extra joints. Replace-the-long-members wins clearly.


6. RECOMMENDATION
-----------------
Family: three sizes on one parameter (rows 4/5/6), identical in X and Z, growing only in Y by inserting a row in front of row G:
S 1,292 x 894 x 1,140 (24 samples), M 1,292 x 1,004 (48), L 1,292 x 1,114 (96). Family L replaces rev L: same stations plus
three places, 15 mm shallower, 18 mm wider, same stiffness and dynamics.
Build first: S, on a bench-depth stand about 600 mm high (top at about 1,740 mm, deck at about 960 mm), or on a 900-deep bench
with no shelf above. Before ordering: run the planner on the S layout (manual steps and exchanges are not yet counted), run the
CAD relocation check on family L, and settle the hazardous liquid temperature (37 C means a thermal module under G0).
Decisions to fix now: items 1-12 of section 3.3, above all: X, Z, the gantry and the head at final size; rows F-B and the
frame front fixed; per-row deck strips and per-bay panels; electronics for L; a connector plane at the back section; markers
on the dock bays; the family placement rules N1-N10 added to the deck checker; the shaker offset fix (also needed in rev L).
Deliberately NOT extendable: width (X beam, X rails, gantry, door, front/back panels), height and Z stroke, the head and its
two carriages, the dock (always 8 bays), the drawer and crate, the control bay. Y rails and Y belts are treated as consumables
that are replaced, never jointed.
